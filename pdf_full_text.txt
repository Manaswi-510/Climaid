1. Project overview 
Purpose 
ClimateAdapt Pune is an AI-assisted climate adaptation action planner for a defined pilot area in Pune. It combines climate, 
geographic, infrastructure, environmental and population data to identify potential vulnerability, explain the evidence 
behind it, recommend practical interventions, compare plans under a budget, and produce a transparent report and 
dashboard. 
Core principle 
The system must distinguish measured or officially reported facts from model forecasts, proxies, assumptions and missing 
data. A map layer being present does not prove that infrastructure is functional; absence of complaint records does not 
mean there were no floods; a model estimate is not a field measurement. 
Pilot scope 
Start with the current Pune pilot bounding box (approximately 18.50–18.60 latitude and 73.80–73.90 longitude). Keep the 
geographic extent configurable so the system can later support another ward, campus, housing society or city. 
Primary users 
Municipal planners, ward officers, campus or society administrators, climate-resilience teams, researchers and community 
organizations. 
Main outputs 
A data-quality summary, vulnerability indicators with evidence, an interactive map, ranked interventions, a budget-aware 
action plan, scenario comparison, downloadable report, and a record of assumptions and data freshness. 
 
2. Feature specification 
A. Geographic project setup 
Allow a user to select a study area using a ward boundary, uploaded GeoJSON, or a configured bounding box. Show the 
study-area boundary, coordinate reference system, area, source and last-updated date. Validate geometry, repair invalid 
polygons where safe, and clip spatial layers to the study area. 
B. Data-source and freshness panel 
For each layer show source, download/retrieval date, period covered, geographic resolution, units, license/attribution, 
processing status and confidence. Flag stale, incomplete, mismatched or unavailable data. Provide a source register and 
data dictionary. 
C. Heat exposure and heat-vulnerability analysis 
Use temperature, apparent temperature, humidity, land-cover/vegetation, built-up context and population where 
compatible data exist. Summarize hot periods and seasonal patterns; map heat-related proxies such as low vegetation 
cover and high built-up density. Keep exposure separate from sensitivity and adaptive capacity. Do not label the result as a 
validated heat-risk prediction without validation data. 
D. Flood and intense-rainfall screening 
Use rainfall totals/probability, elevation/terrain derivatives when available, waterways, drainage infrastructure, 
impervious/built-up proxies and verified historical flood reports if available. Show low-lying areas and drainage proximity 
as screening indicators, not proof of flooding. Mark complaint history and drain condition as missing until supported by 
records.

E. Water-stress and drought screening 
Use rainfall history, evapotranspiration, soil-moisture model estimates, groundwater resource reports and verified water-
supply information. Distinguish district/taluka resource assessments from local monitoring-well measurements. Where 
local groundwater levels are absent, present regional context and clearly label local groundwater status as unavailable. 
F. Green-cover and nature-based resilience 
Use Sentinel-2 imagery when downloaded and appropriate cloud-free coverage is confirmed. Derive NDVI from B08 (near-
infrared) and B04 (red) bands using (B08 - B04) / (B08 + B04). Map vegetation/green-space proxies and OSM parks, 
gardens, grass and woodlands. Explain that NDVI is a vegetation signal, not a direct measure of tree health, shade quality or 
public accessibility. 
G. Population and vulnerable groups 
Use official census or other authoritative population data only when the geographic units match or can be defensibly 
crosswalked. Show total population and available age-group indicators. Never divide city population equally among wards 
or assume historic census boundaries equal current ward boundaries. If a defensible match is not possible, mark the 
statistic unavailable. 
H. Infrastructure and essential services 
Map OSM buildings, roads, schools, hospitals, clinics, drinking-water points, waterways, water bodies, parks and green 
spaces. Include downloaded official/OpenCity stormwater-drain and coverage datasets after inspecting file format, 
metadata and fields. Presence of a drain line or coverage polygon does not establish condition, capacity, blockage status or 
actual service performance. 
I. Evidence-backed explanations 
Every indicator and recommendation should show a human-readable explanation, the input layers used, dates and 
resolution, known gaps, and whether it is an observation, proxy, forecast or assumption. Let users inspect evidence rather 
than presenting an unexplained single score. 
J. Intervention catalogue 
Provide editable interventions such as shade trees/corridors, cool roofs, shaded bus stops/walking routes, rainwater 
harvesting, permeable surfaces, rain gardens, drainage inspection and maintenance, stormwater inlet clearing, 
detention/retention areas, restoration of waterways, water conservation, drought contingency planning and 
protection/restoration of green spaces. Each entry should have applicability conditions, expected benefits, estimated cost 
range, lead time, maintenance needs, responsible stakeholder and evidence source. 
K. Budget-aware action planning 
Let users set a budget, time horizon, constraints, intervention eligibility and priorities. Rank interventions using 
transparent weighted criteria such as risk reduction, population benefit, equity, cost, feasibility, co-benefits and 
maintenance. Provide a simple baseline ranking first; optionally use an optimization solver to choose a portfolio under 
budget and constraints. Display the rationale and trade-offs. 
L. Scenario simulator 
Allow users to change assumptions such as a heatwave, heavy-rainfall scenario, vegetation improvement, water-supply 
restriction or budget change. Compare baseline and proposed actions using clearly labelled proxy indicators. Never imply a 
physically validated impact simulation unless calibrated models and suitable local data exist. 
M. Equity and fairness 
Show which neighborhoods or sectors benefit, where data are missing, and whether the plan directs resources only to 
areas with better data. If population or vulnerability data are incomplete, surface that limitation. Provide configurable 
equity weights and explain how they affect rankings.

N. Interactive dashboard and map 
Provide overview cards, time-series charts, layer toggles, a map legend, filters, data-source details, ward/sector drill-down, 
before/after scenario comparison and an evidence panel. Include accessible colors, readable labels, mobile-friendly layout 
and a clear explanation of uncertainty. 
O. Report generation and exports 
Generate a downloadable PDF and CSV/GeoJSON exports where relevant. The report should include study-area scope, date, 
sources, methods, data-quality notes, indicators, maps/charts, prioritized actions, costs, assumptions, uncertainty, 
limitations and next data-collection steps. 
P. Administration and auditability 
Keep configuration separate from code; log ingestion time and processing outcomes; version indicator definitions and 
action-plan runs; validate uploaded files; protect secrets in environment variables; avoid exposing private data; and retain 
enough metadata to reproduce a result. 
 
3. Data inventory and current collection status 
Ward boundary 
Preferred source: official municipal ward boundaries or a verified boundary file. If unavailable, the pilot bounding box can 
be used temporarily and must be labelled as a study extent, not an official ward boundary. 
OpenStreetMap 
Files already reported saved under data/raw/osm/: parks.geojson, buildings.geojson, roads.geojson, trees.geojson, 
schools.geojson, hospitals.geojson, waterways.geojson, water_bodies.geojson, clinics.geojson, gardens.geojson, 
grass.geojson, woodlands.geojson, drinking_water.geojson. Inspect feature counts, geometry types, timestamps if available 
and coverage before analysis. 
Drainage 
Stormwater-drain resources and ward-level drainage CSVs were reported downloaded from OpenCity. Inspect actual 
filenames, formats, columns, units, spatial coverage, provenance and license before integrating. Do not assume all 
downloaded resources are valid GeoJSON; KML/KMZ or tabular files may need conversion. 
Weather 
Open-Meteo Forecast API test succeeded. The response contained current, hourly and daily variables for coordinates 
18.523726, 73.86876, including temperature, relative humidity, apparent temperature, precipitation, cloud cover, wind, 
evapotranspiration, ET0, soil moisture estimates and UV index. The initial generated request used a different coordinate 
pair; use a documented, intentional pilot point and keep its coordinates in metadata. Forecast data must remain separate 
from historical climate data. 
Population 
An official Census Digital Library 2011 Primary Census Abstract file was found for Pune district. Before use, check sheet 
names, geographic level and whether its ward/village/town units align with the current study area. Do not infer current 
pilot population from a mismatched geography. 
Groundwater 
A Maharashtra Groundwater Resource Estimation report for 2024 was downloaded, and a Pune row was located. Interpret 
values only after confirming the exact column headers and geographic level. Regional resource estimates are not equivalent 
to current groundwater levels in local wells.

5. End-to-end implementation workflow 
Phase 1 — Establish the study area 
Confirm the bounding box or official boundary, coordinate system and pilot metadata. Add a project configuration file for 
area name, extent, timezone, source locations and run date. Validate that all later layers are clipped to the same study area. 
Phase 2 — Inventory and validate raw files 
Create a data manifest listing filename, type, source, retrieval date, coverage, CRS, feature/row count and status. Run 
scripts/validate_data.py to check JSON/GeoJSON validity, CSV/XLSX/PDF presence, empty files, invalid geometries, 
duplicate records, missing fields, coordinate ranges and obvious unit problems. Report issues instead of silently deleting 
data. 
Phase 3 — Fetch weather 
Use scripts/fetch_weather.py to request Open-Meteo data with requests. Save the complete response as 
data/raw/weather/pune_forecast_raw.json, plus hourly and daily CSVs. Include retrieval timestamp, coordinates, timezone 
and data_type='forecast'. Handle HTTP failures, timeouts and malformed responses. Do not describe forecast output as 
historical observations. 
Phase 4 — Inspect OSM and official infrastructure data 
Load each GeoJSON and report counts and geometry types. Inspect OpenCity stormwater resources and drainage CSV 
headers before transforming. Reproject layers to a suitable projected CRS for area/distance calculations; retain source CRS 
metadata. Avoid using latitude/longitude degrees for planar area or distance calculations. 
Phase 5 — Acquire historical climate and environmental layers 
Fetch historical weather separately from a suitable historical API/source and define a meaningful time window (for 
example, multiple years where available). Record data provenance and whether values are reanalysis/modelled or station 
observations. Inspect Sentinel-2 image coverage and clouds; derive NDVI only after downloading the bands. Obtain a 
suitable DEM if terrain-based flood screening is in scope. 
Phase 6 — Population and groundwater validation 
Inspect Census workbook sheets, geography and columns; map records only where a reliable boundary correspondence 
exists. Inspect groundwater report headings and geographic unit before parsing the Pune row. Record unavailable local 
measures as missing. 
Phase 7 — Clean and standardize 
Standardize dates to ISO format, units, column names, null handling and CRS. Deduplicate records where appropriate. 
Preserve raw values and add provenance fields. Distinguish true zero from null/missing. Create a data-quality report. 
Phase 8 — Database and spatial storage 
For a small prototype, GeoJSON/CSV files may be sufficient initially. For multi-layer queries and a production-style app, use 
PostgreSQL with PostGIS. Create tables for study areas, data sources, weather observations/forecasts, infrastructure 
features, population statistics, indicators, interventions, action-plan runs and audit metadata. Add spatial indexes and 
foreign keys. Use migrations or repeatable SQL scripts. 
Phase 9 — Calculate indicators 
Implement reproducible indicator functions in scripts/process_indicators.py. Store formula, input layer versions, spatial 
unit, time window, normalization method, output units and limitations with every indicator. Use min-max or percentile 
scaling only when appropriate and document the reference population. Do not mix incomparable scales without 
normalization. 
Phase 10 — Build the action plan 
In scripts/build_action_plan.py, combine evidence-backed vulnerability indicators with intervention eligibility, cost 
estimates and benefit assumptions. Start with a transparent weighted ranking. Add budget optimization only after inputs

6. Indicator framework 
Heat screening 
Candidate inputs: maximum temperature/apparent temperature, frequency of hot days if historical data support it, 
vegetation/NDVI, built-up density, shade proxies and population exposure. Potential output: heat exposure score and 
separate sensitivity/adaptive-capacity indicators. Do not infer individual health outcomes. 
Flood screening 
Candidate inputs: rainfall intensity/totals, DEM-derived low-lying areas and slope, waterways, drainage 
proximity/coverage and verified flood history. Potential output: screening priority, not a calibrated flood probability unless 
a validated hydrologic/hydraulic model exists. 
Water stress 
Candidate inputs: historical precipitation deficit, ET0, soil moisture estimates, verified water supply information and 
groundwater resource context. Potential output: water-stress screening with a clear distinction between regional and local 
evidence. 
Green-cover deficit 
Candidate inputs: NDVI, land cover, mapped parks/gardens/woodlands and population distribution. Potential output: 
green-cover proxy or access-gap estimate. Do not claim public accessibility without access/entrance data. 
Equity 
Where population or vulnerability indicators are reliable, compare who benefits and whether high-need areas are served. 
Keep a visible warning where demographic data are old, coarse or mismatched. 
Composite score 
If a composite score is used, publish the formula, weights, normalization, missing-data policy and sensitivity to weights. 
Provide component scores beside the composite. Avoid false precision such as reporting several decimal places for weak 
proxies.

7. Intervention catalogue and planning logic 
Heat actions 
Tree/shade corridors; cool-roof pilots; shaded public waiting areas; shade for walking routes; protection of existing tree 
canopy; heat-risk communication. Check water availability, species suitability, utility conflicts, maintenance and land 
ownership. 
Flood actions 
Drain inspection/maintenance; inlet cleaning; rain gardens; permeable surfaces; detention/retention areas; restoration of 
natural drainage corridors; targeted drainage upgrades after engineering review. Do not recommend construction based 
only on proximity or a coarse map. 
Water actions 
Rainwater harvesting; leak detection and repair; water-use efficiency; drought contingency plans; groundwater 
monitoring; recharge measures only after hydrogeological assessment; protection of local water bodies. 
Green/nature actions 
Protect existing green spaces; native planting; restore degraded patches; connect green corridors; create small shade/rest 
areas. Track maintenance and survival rather than counting planting events alone. 
Ranking criteria 
Risk reduction, population served, equity, cost, feasibility, implementation time, co-benefits, maintenance burden, evidence 
strength and reversibility. Weights should be configurable and visible. 
Budget optimization 
Decision variable x_i indicates whether intervention i is selected (or its scale). A simple formulation maximizes total 
weighted benefit subject to sum(cost_i × x_i) <= budget and eligibility/dependency constraints. Benefit and cost estimates 
must be labelled as estimates and should be adjustable. 
Scenario comparison 
Compare baseline and intervention portfolio under the same assumptions. Display change in proxy indicators, budget used, 
people/areas potentially served, confidence and unresolved risks. Avoid presenting proxy deltas as guaranteed real-world 
outcomes.

Map explorer 
Toggle heat, flood-screening, water-stress, green-cover and infrastructure layers. Include legend, opacity controls, click-to-

11. Testing and acceptance criteria 
Ingestion tests 
A valid source downloads and parses; failed requests produce useful errors; original data are retained; timestamps and 
metadata are recorded. 
Geospatial tests 
CRS is recognized; invalid geometries are reported; layers align spatially; area/distance calculations use a projected CRS; 
data are clipped to the pilot area. 
Data-quality tests 
Nulls are not silently converted to zero; duplicates and unexpected units are flagged; forecast and historical data are 
separated; geographic mismatch is detected. 
Indicator tests 
Known sample inputs produce expected outputs; formulas and normalization are versioned; missing input handling is 
deterministic; component scores are inspectable. 
Planner tests 
Selected interventions do not exceed the budget; dependencies and eligibility are respected; rankings respond predictably 
to weight changes; cost assumptions are visible. 
Report/API/UI tests 
Report exports open correctly; API schemas are validated; errors are handled; maps show legends; loading/empty/error 
states are clear; accessibility and basic responsive layout are checked. 
Acceptance definition 
A demonstrable MVP should ingest and validate real sourced data, show at least one reliable indicator and one explicitly 
labelled proxy, explain its evidence, produce a constrained action plan, compare a scenario, and generate a report without 
fabricating missing values. 
 
12. Responsible use, uncertainty and known limitations 
No invented data 
Never create fake drain conditions, flood events, groundwater levels, ward populations or vegetation values to fill gaps. Use 
null/missing and explain what data would resolve the gap. 
No overclaiming 
A forecast is not an observation. A proxy score is not a validated probability. Regional groundwater estimates are not local 
well measurements. OSM completeness varies. Satellite cloud cover must be checked over the study area.

Uncertainty 
Use qualitative confidence labels tied to source quality, freshness, spatial resolution and validation. If a numeric confidence 
score is used, define how it is calculated and validate it. 
Human review 
Engineering decisions such as drainage upgrades, recharge interventions or construction require qualified local 
assessment and approvals. The tool supports planning; it does not replace official warnings or engineering design. 
Privacy and equity 
Use aggregated population data where possible. Do not expose household-level sensitive information. Track which 
communities are underrepresented because of missing data and allow planners to flag those gaps.
